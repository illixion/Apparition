**Apparition is now a development tool.** It supports several anisette sources, including its built-in Android provider, alongside a Mac you own.

**Mac, Windows or Linux**
- **The same app on all three.** Installing Apparition on a device, refreshing, JIT and the companion run the same on macOS, Windows (x64 and ARM64) and Linux (.deb and AppImage, x64 and ARM64), with no Mac involved. Only a free Apple ID's sign-in needs an anisette source. A Mac gives the most complete and most stable setup.
- **Setup for Windows.** Apparition Setup puts the app in place and lets your devices through Windows' firewall, so you are never asked about it. Choose Only me, and the app keeps itself up to date without asking; or Everyone on this PC, which adds a background service that serves your devices with nobody signed in. The zip is still there to unpack anywhere.
- **A start-here guide for each computer** is in the README and on the website: how to get the app, pair an iPhone or iPad over USB (Apple Devices or iTunes on Windows, `usbmuxd` on Linux), and sign in.

**Signing in**
- **Your own Mac is the recommended source.** A Mac on macOS 26 or earlier gives its anisette with no setup and no change to its security, and your other Apple devices get the usual sign-in prompt. Windows and Linux computers, other Macs and your devices pair with it over an encrypted channel, with a six-digit code, and sign in through it.
- **macOS 27: a small VM.** macOS 27 only gives its anisette with SIP and AMFI off, so on Apple silicon Apparition sets up a small macOS 26 virtual machine for it with one button, in the desktop app or with `apparition vm`. The catch: Apple sends a VM's sign-ins a code by SMS only, never a prompt on your devices.
- **Apparition's own Android anisette** works on macOS, Windows and Linux with no Mac at all: choose Apparition's own (Android libraries) when you sign in. It fetches Apple Music's Android libraries from Apple on first use and runs them in the app. It serves sign-ins on that computer; your devices' refreshes still need a Mac's anisette or a third-party server through the companion. Apple has closed this route and reopened it before, so a future update may switch it off.
- **Run your own anisette server with Docker**, on Windows (Docker Desktop or WSL), Linux or macOS: `selfhost/anisette/compose.yaml` is back, pinned to a verified image. It is a sign-in with no Mac and no server from anyone else, with Apple's libraries kept out of Apparition's own process. Give Apparition `http://localhost:6970`.
- **Third-party anisette servers still work**, on every platform, with no Mac at all. Give a server's address in Settings and use Check server. Apparition doesn't supply one, and Apple decides what it accepts.
- **Sign-ins send what Apple's own clients send.** Routing info and the request time now go in every sign-in request, whatever the anisette source; before, a plain server's sign-ins left them out.
- **A verbose record for when Apple changes something.** Settings > Troubleshooting on the device, Companion > Troubleshooting in the desktop app, `apparition --verbose` on the command line, or `setVerboseLog` for an agent writes each request a sign-in sends to Apple and the answer to `apparition-verbose.log`, as structure and sizes: no passwords, tokens or anisette values. Off unless you turn it on, and a file of its own, so it never ends up in the ordinary log.
- **Two-factor sign-in is sturdier.** It offers SMS when Apple answers with phone options, uses a code Apple has already texted, and keeps a sign-in log across launches.
- **Your device finds your computer by itself**, on your Wi-Fi at home and over Tailscale away, so the weekly refresh and JIT keep working wherever the device is.

**For developers**
- **Install apps or Develop apps.** The desktop app asks which on first run, and you can switch from its header at any time. Develop adds Apps, Debug and Builds tabs, a readiness checklist, and a pick-a-folder way to add an Xcode project.
- **The Apparition Store is built into the Mac app.** It builds and deploys your apps and keeps the builds, collects crash reports and DebugTrace traces, serves the debug console and an MCP server for agents, and needs nothing installed beside the app. It runs on Windows and Linux too.
- **Debug a running app.** Each running app has Tune, Log, Profile and Memory buttons. Values you tune become sliders, and crashes arrive symbolicated from the exact build on the device.
- **`apparition build`** builds with Xcode, signs, installs over Apparition's own pairing and launches, using Xcode's tunnel (`devicectl`) only as a fallback. It builds Mac apps too, and signs live-patch libraries.
- **`apparition launch`** starts an app with its console attached, and with JIT if you ask. **`apparition inject`** patches Swift into the running app, relaunching or attaching by itself when it has to.
- **Build from another computer.** Pair a Windows or Linux computer, or another Mac, with your Mac for developer access. Its builds go to your devices with `apparition deploy`, and its agents debug your apps through the Mac. Without a Mac, xtool builds on Linux (and Windows through WSL) and `apparition deploy` installs the result.
- **Also new:** an Updates view and `apparition updates` for keeping apps current, `apparition crash-reports` for reading what crashed, and `apparition container` to move an app's data off a device and back.

**Installing and updating**
- **Updates close the app first.** Installing an update of a running app on a headset or phone closes it, as Xcode does, so the update applies at once instead of waiting for you to close the app. That covers installs from your Mac and from Apparition's own Library on the device.
- **Apparition updates itself the same way.** After a refresh you start, it asks "Close Apparition to finish updating?" and closes on your answer. A refresh the system starts closes it by itself when nobody is using it. Apparition never closes while an app is running under its JIT debugger.
- **`build-and-sign` and `apparition build` pick their route.** `--via auto` (the default) installs through Apparition's own pairing and falls back to Xcode's tunnel (`devicectl`) only when the device can't be reached that way. `--via devicectl` forces the tunnel, and `--via apparition` never falls back.
- **A dropped connection during an update is no longer a failure.** When the device holds an update until the app closes, the install is reported as queued.
- **The device finds your computer by name again** and lists it once, however many networks it hears it on. It also no longer saves the address of a virtual machine's bridge (the anisette VM's or Docker's), which it can never reach.

**Interface**
- Tabs and the setup steps ease in, and the header's three pills share one height.
- The header badge says where anisette comes from in one short line (`Anisette · VM`) with a status dot: green for a Mac's anisette, amber for Android or a server, red when sign-ins and the companion disagree, hollow when only this computer's sign-ins use it. Sign-ins and the companion use one anisette source.
- The Companion tab can offer Apparition's own Android anisette to your devices, which suits a Mac with 8 GB that has no room for the anisette VM.

**Fixes**
- The Companion tab no longer scrolls back to the top of an error every two seconds.
- A third-party anisette server that doesn't answer no longer hangs a sign-in, and one that answers without the headers Apple checks says so.
